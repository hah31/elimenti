Website redesign for independent businesses

A website redesign with Elimenti replaces a slow, dated or hard-to-update site with a new one at the same domain. We review what you have, redesign it, rewrite the copy and rebuild it, on WordPress by default or custom-coded. Old page addresses are redirected to their new homes, and your email isn't touched. The redesign, step by step

1. Review
We go through the current site: speed, structure, what it runs on and, where Search Console data exists, which pages bring visitors.
2. Page list
Every existing address goes into one list, marked keep, merge or retire. You approve it before design starts.
3. New design
The whole site designed fresh, not the old layout in new colors.
4. Rewritten copy
We rewrite each page from a conversation with you. Facts that still hold stay.
5. Build
On WordPress by default, so you can edit it afterwards, or custom-coded if it rarely changes. It's built privately while the old site stays live.
6. Redirects
Each old address that changes gets a permanent redirect to the page that replaced it, so bookmarks and links from other sites still work.
7. Domain and email
The domain stays with its current registrar. At launch we change only the DNS records that send visitors to the website. The mail records are left as they are.
8. Phone testing
Each page checked on real phones: forms, the tap-to-call number and load time.
9. Launch
The domain is pointed at the new site, every redirect is tested and the new sitemap is submitted to Google.

What moving off each platform involves

  • Wix. Wix says a Wix site has to run on its own servers, so it can't be moved as it is. The new site is built separately and the domain is pointed to it.
  • Squarespace. Squarespace can export some pages and one blog as a file WordPress can import. Style settings and store pages don't export. The design is new anyway, so we use the export for blog posts and move the rest by hand.
  • Joomla. We copy the content worth keeping across page by page and record each old address for the redirect list.
  • An aging WordPress theme. You stay on WordPress. Posts, pages and media carry over, and the old theme is replaced.
  • Static HTML. Old addresses often end in .html. Each one is redirected, and on WordPress you gain a login to edit the site.

Of the 122 local business websites that loaded in our phone test, 49% ran on WordPress, 8% on Squarespace and 5% on Wix. Still deciding? Read Wix, Squarespace, WordPress or custom.

How search rankings are protected

We follow Google's own instructions for moving a site and for redirects.

  • Map old addresses to new ones. Google's guide starts with a list of current URLs and where each should go. That's step two.
  • Use permanent, server-side redirects. Google treats a permanent redirect as a signal that the new page should replace the old one in results. It also states that 301 and other permanent redirects don't cause a loss in PageRank.
  • Don't send everything to the homepage. Google warns that pointing many old URLs at one unrelated page can be treated as a soft 404. Each redirect goes to the closest matching page.
  • Leave redirects in place. Google recommends at least one year. We don't remove them.
  • Change as little else as possible. Google advises against changing several things at once. A redesign already changes the design and often the platform, so the domain stays the same and addresses stay as they are wherever they can.

Google also tells site owners to expect rankings to fluctuate for a while, and says a small or medium site can take a few weeks for most pages to be processed. Nobody outside Google controls rankings, so we don't promise positions. We promise the steps above.

Questions about redesigns

Will I lose my Google rankings in a redesign?

A planned move protects them as far as anyone can. Google states that permanent redirects don't lose PageRank, and it tells owners to expect some temporary movement while it recrawls the site. We redirect every changed address and keep the pages that bring visits. No designer can promise a ranking, and we don't.

Will my domain and email keep working?

Yes. The domain stays with its current registrar, and we only change where it points. Email is delivered according to the domain's MX records, and launching a website doesn't require changing them. If your mailboxes sit on the old web host itself, we flag that early and plan the email move with you before that hosting is cancelled.

Do I have to change platforms?

No. A WordPress site can be redesigned and stay on WordPress. If you're happy on Wix or Squarespace, a new template and new photos may be all you need, which is a refresh. We build on WordPress and in custom code, so a redesign with us lands on one of those two.

Can you reuse my existing copy and photos?

Yes, where they still do their job. Photos you own are resized and compressed for the new layout. Copy is rewritten by default, since it's included, and accurate details carry over.

Will my site go offline during the switch?

Not if the switch is done in order. The old site stays up while the new one is built and tested at a private address. Launch is a change to the domain's DNS settings, and the old hosting is kept until the new site is answering for everyone.
