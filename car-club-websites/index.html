Car club website design

A car club website should let the cars do the talking, grow as the collection grows and screen new members through a real application. Elimenti designs them for modern and private clubs anywhere in the US, built on WordPress so the club can add to it, or custom-coded. We built the site for 1 of 1 Car Club, a private Porsche club, and our founder co-founded Red Devils Garage, a local car club.

What a private club's site needs

  • Say what the club is, and what it isn't. Serious members want to know if they fit before they apply.
  • Show the cars. A collection or member-car page that's easy to add to as the club grows.
  • An application that asks the right questions. What they drive, where they are, why they want in.
  • Privacy where it matters. Invitation-only clubs decide what stays off the public site, and the design should respect that.

Applications that do the screening

For a club that admits people one at a time, the application is the most important page on the site. It should ask what the club actually decides on, and nothing it doesn't.

  • The car. Make, model and year, plus a photo if the club wants to see it.
  • The city. So the club knows which drives and meets the person would actually make.
  • Who sent them. For an invitation-only club, the member who put their name forward.
  • Why they want in. One open question, read by a person, tells a club more than a page of checkboxes.

Applications go to the club's inbox, never to a public list. The page should also say what happens after someone hits send: who reads it, when they can expect to hear back and that not everyone is accepted. Saying it up front saves a round of awkward emails later.

Member cars, shown the way members want

  • One entry per car. Details and photos go in once, and the page lays itself out. Adding a car shouldn't mean designing a page.
  • Owners named only if they say so. Showing the car doesn't have to mean naming the person who owns it.
  • Plates and VINs kept out of photos. Worth a second look before any photo goes up.
  • A clean card for a car with no photos yet. On 1 of 1 Car Club's site, it shows the year in large type with the model beneath, so a new arrival can go up the day it comes in.
  • Room for cars that have left. A list of past cars keeps the club's history when the collection changes.

Keeping things private

Privacy on a club site is mostly about what never goes on it.

  • The member list. A public directory is the first thing to leave out. If members need to find each other, a members-only page with individual logins does it.
  • Where cars live. No home addresses, and no photos that give away a garage or a street.
  • Start points for private drives. Post the date and the general area, and send the exact meeting spot to the people who've signed up.
  • Guests in event photos. Faces and plates of people who didn't agree to be on the site stay off it.

Some clubs want a members-only area for event details, dues or documents; on WordPress, each member gets their own login and those pages stay behind it. Others keep the whole site public and put nothing on it that needs protecting. Either way, it's decided at the start, not after launch.

Where templates fall short for clubs

  • Templates built for gyms and nonprofits. Membership templates assume a sign-up button and a price table. A club that admits members by invitation needs the opposite.
  • Collections that don't grow. If every new car means laying out a new page by hand, the collection stops getting updated.
  • Events that disappear. Meets and drives get posted on social media and vanish. A site keeps the date, place, rules and registration link in one place, and the photos afterward.

Questions clubs ask

How do people apply to join?

Through an application form on the site that asks what the club needs to know: name, city, the cars they own and why they want in. Submissions go straight to the club's inbox. We built exactly that for 1 of 1 Car Club.

Can we keep our member list off the public site?

Yes, and for a private club we'd recommend it. Members can be shown through their cars without names, and if members need a directory, it goes on a members-only page behind individual logins. What's public and what isn't is settled before design starts.

Can we add cars to the collection ourselves?

On a WordPress site, yes. You get your own login, and at launch we walk you through adding a car: the details, the photos and where it shows up. On a custom-coded site like 1 of 1 Car Club's, the collection runs from a single data file, so a new car is one entry plus photos. Send the details through Site Care and it's live within five business days.

Do you work with clubs outside Chicago?

Yes. Every project runs over video calls, and we take projects anywhere in the US. 1 of 1 Car Club is based in Chicago and Dallas, with events in other cities.
