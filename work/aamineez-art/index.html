Aamineez Art: portfolio website

Elimenti designed and custom-coded aamineez.art, a one-page portfolio for Aamineez, an artist who works in Arabic calligraphy and alcohol ink. It was a full build with one goal: turn a link shared in a local group chat into commission inquiries.

The live site, captured November 4, 2026.

The artist and the audience

Aamineez began studying Arabic calligraphy in 2020, with lessons in the Thuluth script, and pairs it with alcohol ink painting. The pieces are made to hang in homes, and the site was built to share the work with the artist's own local community.

Someone in that community who likes a piece doesn't need persuading. They need to see the work clearly and know how to ask about it.

The problem

The launch was a single message in a large local group chat. People would tap the link on their phones, between other messages, and decide in a few seconds whether the work was worth asking about. Anything that slowed that down, like a loading screen, a menu to figure out or a contact form to fill in, would cost an inquiry.

What we did, and why

  • Put the work first. The page opens on the gallery, not on an introduction. Each piece shows its title, year and medium, with a Sold label where it applies, so a visitor can see what's available without asking.
  • Made every piece readable up close. Tapping a piece opens a full-screen viewer with more photos of it, its size and, where the piece carries text, an English translation. A buyer who doesn't read Arabic can see what the piece says before asking about it.
  • Kept the words short. An about section in the artist's own voice: how the practice started and what the work combines.
  • Removed the friction. One "Inquire on WhatsApp" button, always on screen, that opens a chat with a first line already written. Asking about a commission takes one tap.
  • Stayed quiet. A minimal layout in neutral colors that lets the calligraphy carry the page.

The viewer on a phone: photo, medium, size and translation, with arrows to the next image.

The takeaway

When a site's whole job is turning a quick look into a conversation, fewer features win. One gallery, a short about section in the artist's voice and one button that starts the conversation did more than a full site would have. The translation in the viewer is the detail that matters most: it answers the question a buyer would otherwise have to ask.
