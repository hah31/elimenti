1 of 1 Car Club: website design and build

Elimenti planned, wrote, designed and custom-coded the website for 1 of 1 Car Club, a private, invitation-only Porsche club based in Chicago and Dallas. The site had one job: show serious Porsche people what the club is, then let the application do the filtering.

The club site as launched, captured in September 2026.

The club and its members

The club is built around its founder's private Porsche collection, and membership is by invitation. The people it's written for already know why a 993 GT2 R matters. Nobody arrives at this site needing Porsche explained to them.

That shaped everything. A site written for collectors can skip the introductions, and it has to: the fastest way to lose this audience is to tell them something they already know.

The problem

Three things had to be true at once:

  • It couldn't be about the founder. The cars and the club do the talking, and the founder's name isn't on the site.
  • It had to grow without a rebuild. The collection changes. Cars arrive, and some leave. Adding one couldn't mean paying a developer to redesign a page.
  • It had to filter, not sell. An invitation-only club doesn't want more applicants. It wants the right ones.

What we did, and why

  • Wrote the whole site before designing any of it. The first deliverable was a site plan in words only: every page, every section, every headline. Design started once the words were right, because a layout designed before the words exist gets rebuilt when they arrive.
  • Cut hard. Short, direct copy that says what the club is and what it isn't. The Philosophy page shipped at about 40 percent of its first draft.
  • Built the collection to run from one file. The Collection page reads every car from a single data file. Adding a car means filling in one entry and dropping photos in a folder. The grid, the featured stories and the list of past cars all update on their own.
  • Made room for photos that don't exist yet. A car without photos shows a typographic card, with the year in large type and the model beneath it, instead of a gray box or a broken image. A new car can go up the day it arrives and get its photos later.
  • Covered American Speed. A section on the collection's two cars in production for American Speed, the Amazon MGM Studios film about the Whittington brothers.
  • Set the look. Navy, red and light blue drawn from classic Porsche racing liveries, on white and cool gray, with modern type.
  • Built the application. Name, city, the Porsches they own and why they want in, sent straight to the club's inbox. The questions do the first round of screening.

How it was checked before launch

The launch checklist covered the domain, email forwarding, a live test of the application form and a full pass on a real iPhone in Safari, not just a desktop browser imitating one: the menu, the stacked layouts, tap-target sizes and whether text reads without zooming. Members open a link like this on their phones first.

The takeaway

A site for a private club is mostly about what you leave out. No founder's name and no hard sell. The cars do the talking, the copy says what the club is and isn't, and the application does the filtering. Putting the collection in one data file means adding a car is a quick edit to one file, not a redesign.
