Squarespace or a custom website: which fits a high-end business?

Squarespace is cheaper: its Core and Plus plans come to about $1,044 to $1,764 over three years, plus your time. A custom website, designed for your business and built on WordPress or custom-coded, costs more up front, from $4,000 with us. Choose Squarespace if you'll build it yourself on a small budget; choose a custom site if it has to prove quality.

By Hashim H., founder of Elimenti. Published November 4, 2026.

The short answer

Choose Squarespace if you're just starting out, you want to build the site yourself, or you need a store and a site on one bill. Choose WordPress if you or your staff will update the site often, and you want it designed around your business and able to move to any host. Choose a custom-coded site if it rarely changes, it needs to feel as sharp as possible on a phone, and you're happy to hand every change to someone else.

A note on bias: we build custom sites, on WordPress and in custom code, so we have a stake in this answer. We've tried to give Squarespace its due, and if it's the better fit for you, we'll say so on the first call.

What Squarespace does well

  • Speed to launch. Pick a template, add your words and photos, and you can be live in a weekend.
  • A predictable bill. Plans run $19 to $99 a month billed yearly, with hosting included.
  • No upkeep. Squarespace handles hosting, security and software updates.
  • You edit it. Changing a price or adding a photo takes minutes and costs nothing extra.
  • Selling online. Commerce is built in, which matters if online sales are a real part of the business.

What a designed site has over a template

This part is the same for WordPress and custom code, as long as the site is designed for you.

  • It looks like you. A template was designed before anyone knew your business existed. Customers who notice details notice when your site shares a layout with thousands of others.
  • It's built around how your customers decide. A med spa needs a page per treatment, a builder needs projects sorted by type, a designer needs project stories. The structure fits the business instead of the other way around.

Where WordPress fits

  • You edit it. Text, photos, events and posts, with your own login, as often as you like.
  • A plugin for almost anything. Memberships, event calendars, forms and much more come from a huge ecosystem of plugins.
  • It moves with you. WordPress is open-source software that runs on any host that supports it, and any WordPress developer can take the site over. Squarespace's export, by comparison, is a WordPress-format file that leaves out your styles and custom CSS, store pages, most page types other than standard pages, galleries and one blog, and content in page-specific headers and footers.
  • The tradeoff: upkeep. WordPress, the theme and the plugins need regular updates, and the site needs hosting that can run WordPress. A care plan or a developer usually handles both.

Where a custom-coded site fits

  • It's very fast. A custom-coded site loads only what the page needs. Platform sites load the platform's scripts on every page, whether you use them or not.
  • Nothing to update. There's no platform, plugin or dashboard to keep current.
  • You own the files. A custom-coded site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript you can take to any host or developer.
  • No platform fees. Static custom sites can run on low-cost or free hosting. You pay for the build and for changes, not a monthly subscription.
  • The tradeoff: you don't edit it. Every change goes through a developer or a care plan.

Squarespace figures use its published annual-billing prices and don't include a designer, if you hire one, or transaction fees on sales. Premium WordPress plugins or themes, if a site needs them, are paid by the owner on top. The honest read: a designed site costs more. What you pay for is a site built for your business, and either the ability to edit it yourself or someone who makes every change for you.

The middle option: a designer on Squarespace

Many designers customize Squarespace templates for clients. You get a better-looking site than you'd build alone, and you can still edit it yourself. You also keep the platform's limits: its page structures, its scripts on every page and its export rules. For a business that wants to edit often on a small budget, it's a sensible compromise.

Five questions that decide it

  • How often will the site change, and who changes it? Weekly, by you, on a site you build yourself: Squarespace. Often, by you or your staff, on a site designed for you: WordPress. Rarely, by someone else: custom-coded.
  • Do customers judge you on taste? Boutiques, designers, builders, med spas and firms with premium prices usually do, which points to a designed site on WordPress or in custom code.
  • Do you sell products online? If online sales are central, use a commerce platform, and link your website to it if you want both.
  • Who will write the words? A template doesn't write copy. Many studios include it, and ours does.
  • What's your budget this year? If $4,000 is out of reach, start on Squarespace and move later. Your domain comes with you.
