Squarespace or a custom website: which fits a high-end business?

Squarespace is the right choice for many businesses, and a custom-coded site is the right choice for others. This guide explains where each one wins, what each really costs over three years and how to tell which one fits yours.

By Hashim H., founder of Elimenti. Published November 4, 2026.

The short answer

Choose Squarespace if you want to edit the site yourself every week, you're just starting out, or you need a store and a site on one bill. Choose a custom-coded site if your customers judge you on taste and quality, your site doesn't change daily, and you want it to look like your business rather than a template.

A note on bias: we build custom-coded sites, so we have a stake in this answer. We've tried to give Squarespace its due, and if it's the better fit for you, we'll say so on the first call.

What Squarespace does well

  • Speed to launch. Pick a template, add your words and photos, and you can be live in a weekend.
  • A predictable bill. Plans run $19 to $99 a month billed yearly, with hosting included.
  • No upkeep. Squarespace handles hosting, security and software updates.
  • You edit it. Changing a price or adding a photo takes minutes and costs nothing extra.
  • Selling online. Commerce is built in, which matters if online sales are a real part of the business.

Where a custom-coded site pulls ahead

  • It looks like you. A template was designed before anyone knew your business existed. Customers who notice details notice when your site shares a layout with thousands of others.
  • It's built around how your customers decide. A med spa needs a page per treatment, a builder needs projects sorted by type, a designer needs project stories. Custom structure fits the business instead of the other way around.
  • It's fast. A custom-coded site loads only what the page needs. Platform sites load the platform's scripts on every page, whether you use them or not.
  • You own the files. A custom-coded site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript you can take to any host or developer. Squarespace's export is a WordPress-format file that leaves out your styles and custom CSS, store pages, most page types other than standard pages, galleries and one blog, and content in page-specific headers and footers.
  • No platform fees. Static custom sites can run on low-cost or free hosting. You pay for the build and for changes, not a monthly subscription.

The middle option: a designer on Squarespace

Many designers customize Squarespace templates for clients. You get a better-looking site than you'd build alone, and you can still edit it yourself. You also keep the platform's limits: its page structures, its scripts on every page and its export rules. For a business that wants to edit often, it's a sensible compromise.

Five questions that decide it

  • How often will the site change? Weekly, by you: Squarespace. A few times a month, by someone else: custom works.
  • Do customers judge you on taste? Boutiques, designers, builders, med spas and firms with premium prices usually do.
  • Do you sell products online? If online sales are central, use a commerce platform, and link a custom site to it if you want both.
  • Who will write the words? A template doesn't write copy. Most custom studios, including ours, include it.
  • What's your budget this year? If $3,000 is out of reach, start on Squarespace and move later. Your domain comes with you.
