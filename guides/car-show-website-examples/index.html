Car show website examples: what real event sites get right

The best car show websites answer two people fast: the owner deciding whether to enter a car, and the spectator deciding whether to come. Below are seven real event sites, from Pebble Beach to a Sunday-morning gathering in downtown Hinsdale, with what each one does well and one thing it misses. Every site was checked on November 18, 2026.

By Hashim H., Elimenti. Published November 18, 2026.

Who's looking at these

I've run a car event myself. As a co-founder of Red Devils Garage, a local car club, I organized its charity car meet in the Hinsdale Central High School parking lot: more than 200 cars, and $10,000 raised for HCS Family Services. At Elimenti, I make targeted edits to Burdi Clothing's existing website, including the pages for its Father's Day and Toys for Tots car shows. We haven't built a full website for a car show or a concours, so I read these as an organizer and as someone who writes event pages.

The events below aren't ranked. They're grouped by size, because Pebble Beach and a monthly cars-and-coffee ask different things of a website.

Major concours

Multi-day events with ticket tiers, auctions and a year-round audience. Their sites have to serve entrants, spectators, sponsors and the press at once.

Pebble Beach Concours d'Elegance

pebblebeachconcours.net

What it does well: The results outlive the weekend. The 2026 winners have pages of their own: Best of Show, the nominees, special awards and first, second and third in every class, each with the car, the owner and the owner's home town. Another page lists every Best of Show winner back to 1950. The Entrants page gives an owner the whole year: applications go out in November and are due at the start of January, the selection committee meets in February, and most applicants hear by April. The 2027 class list is already up.

What it misses: The Volunteers page still opens with last year. It says applications for the 2026 Concours "are now closed," three months after that show. What a reader needs in November, that 2027 applications open at the end of January, comes after it.

The Amelia Concours

ameliaconcours.com

What it does well: The first screen is the dates, March 4 to 7, 2027, and two buttons: Purchase tickets and 2027 Schedule. The schedule is split by day, with times. Plan Your Visit covers what people ask the week before: driving directions from the north and the south, which lot is for spectators and when it opens, what can't come through the gate, and whether strollers and mobility scooters are allowed on the show field. They are.

What it misses: The results are hard to find. The site has a winners gallery, headed 2026 Winners, with the Best in Show cars and the class awards. None of the pages we opened link to it, in the menu or the footer. We only found it through the site's sitemap file.

Regional concours

One-day shows run by a committee and a lot of volunteers, usually for a charity. Fewer pages, but the same questions from owners and spectators.

Geneva Concours d'Elegance

genevaconcours.net

What it does well: One paragraph on the homepage answers a spectator. The show, in Geneva, Illinois, was Sunday, August 23, 2026, from 10 to 4 on Third Street downtown, free to the public, benefiting the LivingWell Cancer Resource Center. A list of links under it covers the rest: the nomination form for owners, car club registration, a map, the city's public parking lots, hotels and places to eat. A weekend calendar gives the day by the hour: show cars enter at 6:30, judging starts at 9, the public arrives at 10 and awards are at 2:30. The Gallery keeps the trophy recipients from past years.

What it misses: The Feature Marque page fell a year behind. Under a header dated August 23, 2026, it still describes what the "2025 Geneva Concours d'Elegance will include."

Keeneland Concours d'Elegance

keenelandconcours.com

What it does well: The Entrants page for this Lexington, Kentucky, show reads like it was written by someone who has parked a show field. Check-in opens at 6:30, cars are in position by 8:30, judging starts at 9 and each car gets 20 minutes. A car without a fire extinguisher loses five points. Every car needs two wheel chocks, and the show lends them. Chairs are fine, tents aren't, and it runs rain or shine. The homepage lists the 2026 winner in each class, names the beneficiary, the University of Kentucky's Golisano Children's Hospital, and shows $400,000 donated to date.

What it misses: The class years don't match between pages. The Entrants page defines Class 1 as antiques through 1924 and Class 2 as 1925 to 1945. The winners list on the homepage has Class 1 through 1920 and Class 2 as 1921 to 1942. The owner of a 1923 car can't tell which class it belongs in.

Hilton Head Island Concours d'Elegance

hhiconcours.com

What it does well: The exhibitor page tells an owner what to gather before starting the application: engine details, the VIN, proof of insurance, the car's story, which becomes the sign beside it on the show field, and six photos. Each required angle is listed, with a note that .heic files won't be accepted. It gives the deadline, August 7, 2027, and says cars shown in 2025 or 2026 aren't eligible. The 2026 winners are posted, next to the 2025 ones.

What it misses: The 2027 dates, November 5 to 7, appear only as lettering inside the banner at the top of the homepage. They aren't written as text anywhere on that page, so a screen reader can't read them out.

Cars-and-coffee and charity shows

Free, short and run on goodwill. These sites don't need ticket tiers. They need the next date, the parking plan and the rules that keep the venue.

Caffeine and Octane

caffeineandoctane.com

What it does well: The show page answers a first-timer before they leave the house. It's the first Sunday of every month, 9 to 11:30, at Town Center at Cobb in Kennesaw, Georgia, free for spectators and for show cars, with no registration. It says which gate takes which cars, that the Central Lot needs you there by 8:30 and lets nobody out before 11:30, and where spectators park. The rules are blunt: no revving, no burnouts, no loud music. And it gives the reason, which is keeping the mall as the venue.

What it misses: The homepage doesn't say when the show is. The day and hours are one click down, under Show Details, and we didn't find the date of the next show written on either page.

Fuelfed Coffee & Classics

fuelfed.com

What it does well: A club's Sunday-morning gatherings of European classics in Winnetka, Hinsdale and other towns, with spectators welcome. Its Hinsdale page is written for the person driving in. It lists the three 2026 Sundays, each with a featured theme, from 9 to 11 at Chicago and Burlington Avenues. Drivers are told to enter southbound on Garfield, because there's no left turn from the northbound side, and spectators are sent to the Metra commuter lot nearby. Owners are asked to print a windshield sheet with the car's details, so visitors can read about a car when its driver has wandered off. The events calendar shows the date it was last updated.

What it misses: The cutoff year changes from page to page. The page that explains the series says members show European classics from 1995 and older. The Hinsdale and Winnetka pages say 2000 and older.

What the good ones have in common

  • They put the date in words where you land. The Amelia's first screen is its dates and a ticket button. Geneva does it in one paragraph.
  • They tell owners how to get in and when they'll hear. Pebble Beach gives the calendar from application to decision. Hilton Head lists what to have ready.
  • They write the field rules down. Keeneland's fire extinguisher rule and Caffeine and Octane's no-revving rule are on the page, not just on a sign at the gate.
  • They tell each kind of visitor where to park. Show cars one way, spectators another, the way Fuelfed and Caffeine and Octane do.
  • They keep the results. Winners by class with names, and past years left online.
  • They name the cause. Geneva and Keeneland both say who benefits on the first page you open.
  • They send payments to a tool built for it. Keeneland's Tickets button opens a separate ticket site, so the event's own pages never have to take a card.
  • They keep every page on the same year. Half the misses above are a page left on last year, or one page disagreeing with another. A yearly event needs a yearly pass through every page.

How we looked at these sites

We opened each site on November 18, 2026: the homepage, plus the entry, visitor and results pages described above. Five were viewed on a desktop screen and as a phone 390 pixels wide. Geneva's was viewed at phone width, and Fuelfed's pages were read as text only, so we haven't judged how either looks on a desktop. Everything above is what those public pages showed that day, and sites change. None of these events is a client, and none of them asked to be included.
