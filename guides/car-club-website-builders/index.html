Car club website builders and membership software, compared

A small local club that needs dues, a roster and an events page is usually well served by an all-in-one platform. RumbleSeat, Middletwin, MemberDay and ClubExpress publish starting prices between $0 and $29 a month. A designed site earns its cost when the public site is the club's first impression, or when an established club has chapters and archives to carry. Below is what each option charges and covers, taken from the vendors' own pages on November 18, 2026.

By Hashim H., Elimenti. Published November 18, 2026.

Who wrote this, and what we haven't done

Elimenti is a web design studio, so read this knowing we sell one of the options: club sites on WordPress and in custom code. We built the site for 1 of 1 Car Club, a private Porsche club in Chicago and Dallas, and I co-founded Red Devils Garage, a local car club.

We have not built a site on any membership platform listed here, and we haven't run a club on one. Every price and feature below is the vendor's published information as of the day we checked. Plenty of clubs don't need a studio at all, and this guide says which.

Which one fits your club

A small local club: dues, a roster and an events page

Picture 40 to 150 members, yearly dues, a monthly meet and one show a summer. The club needs to collect dues and nudge renewals, share one roster among officers and post each event with a date, a time and an address. An all-in-one platform does all of that.

This club does not need a custom site, and it does not need us. Choose by what you collect online. A flat fee like Middletwin's or MemberDay's is easy to budget. If little money moves online, RumbleSeat's Standard plan or MembershipWorks' free plan costs nothing until it does.

A private or invitation-based club

Here the public site has a different job. A stranger decides in seconds whether the club is serious, and the club decides who gets in. Some platforms can hold a request until an officer approves it. RumbleSeat, for one, lists invitations and approvals. If that and a template are enough, use them.

A designed site is worth paying for when the look carries weight and the application must ask the club's own questions: the car, the city, who referred them and why they want in. On the 1 of 1 Car Club site, the application lands in the club's inbox, and the site connects to no membership platform.

An established marque or classic club

A club with chapters, decades of back issues and a magazine hits page limits and template layouts first. It usually keeps its records in a membership system already. The usual answer is a WordPress site for the public pages, the archive and chapter news, connected to that existing system for joining, renewing and the directory.

Vendors plan for this: WildApricot says its widgets embed in other websites and that it integrates with WordPress. MembershipWorks publishes a WordPress plugin. Join It includes an embeddable purchase widget from its Total plan up. An all-in-one can carry a big club too: RumbleSeat says the Classic Car Club of America runs its site, events and registry there.

What pairing a designed site with membership software means

Pairing means two systems with a clear line between them. The designed site holds what the public sees: the club's story, the cars, the calendar, chapter pages and the archive. The membership platform holds what's private: the roster, payments, renewal reminders and the directory behind each member's login.

On the site, the Join, Renew and Member login buttons hand off to the platform. Sometimes that's a link to the platform's own page. Sometimes the platform's form sits inside the site, through a widget or plugin. Either way, the treasurer works in one place and no record gets typed twice.

The trade-offs are two bills, two logins for officers and platform pages that won't always match the site's design. The handoff differs by vendor, so we look at a club's system before writing a proposal.

What a club's website costs over a year

Take a club of 60 members that collects $50 in dues from each online, $3,000 in all. The figures are the vendors' published ones. The arithmetic is ours.

  • A flat platform fee. Middletwin at 51 to 150 members is $20 a month, or $240 a year. MemberDay Core with the website builder is $428 a year. ClubExpress at 42¢ a member is $25.20 a month, or $302.40 a year, plus $150 for setup in year one. WildApricot at 100 contacts, prepaid, is $59.40 a month, or $712.80 a year.
  • Or a percentage. From January 2027, RumbleSeat Standard charges no subscription and 4.9% of payments: $147 on $3,000. Join It Starter, billed yearly, is $313.20 plus 3%, which is $90 here.
  • Card processing. Stripe's standard rate is 2.9% plus 30¢ for each successful domestic card payment. That's $1.75 on a $50 payment, or $105 across 60 members. ClubExpress lists 2.99% plus 30¢ on its Basic payment model.
  • A domain. Shopify's 2026 cost guide puts registration at $1 to $20-plus a year.

For this club, a year on a platform comes to roughly $250 to $820 with card fees, plus the domain.

A designed site is a separate cost on top. The same Shopify guide, citing a 2026 GoodFirms survey, reports that 63% of agencies quoted between $1,000 and $15,000 for custom design and development. We don't publish a figure for our own work: every project is scoped individually, and the price comes in a written proposal. How a project works explains the steps.

Common questions

What is the cheapest way to put a car club online?

Three published options cost little or nothing to start. MembershipWorks is free for up to 50 accounts, without event registration. RumbleSeat has no subscription now, or on Standard from 2027, and takes a percentage of payments instead. Middletwin is $10 a month for up to 50 members.

Does a small car club need a custom website?

No. A club that needs dues, a roster and an events page can run on an all-in-one platform for years. A custom site starts to matter when the public site has to impress people before they apply, or when chapters and archives outgrow the platform's pages.

Do these platforms take a cut of dues?

Some do. Join It charges 1.5% to 3% by plan. RumbleSeat charges 1% today, and 4.9% or 1% by plan from 2027. MemberPress adds 4.9% on its lowest plan, and Squarespace charges 0% to 7% on membership sales. Middletwin, MemberDay and MembershipWorks say they add nothing. Card processing applies either way.

Can we keep our membership software and still get a new website?

Usually, yes. The new site carries the public pages, and its join, renew and login buttons hand off to the system the club already uses. Check whether the vendor offers a widget, a plugin or at least a stable link to its join page.
