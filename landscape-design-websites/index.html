Websites for landscape design firms

A landscape design firm's website should show finished landscapes the way you'd walk a client through them, explain how design, build and care fit together, and book the first site visit. Elimenti designs them for firms in Hinsdale, Burr Ridge, Oak Brook and the western suburbs, on WordPress or custom-coded. Projects start at $4,000.

What a landscape design website needs to do

  • Projects told as problems solved. A sloped Burr Ridge lot, a narrow Hinsdale side yard, a new build with no trees. Each project page names the site problem, the plan and the materials, with photos across seasons if you have them.
  • Design, build and care, kept apart. A homeowner planning a full redesign and one looking for seasonal care want different things. Each gets its own pages and its own short form.
  • The process and the calendar. Site visit, design, proposal, install, care. Say when design work happens and how early spring installs fill, so the call in March isn't a surprise.
  • A plain answer on design fees. If design is billed separately or credited toward the install, say so before the first visit.
  • Your service area by town. Name the towns you actually work in. Homeowners and builders search by town, and it keeps calls inside your radius.
  • Credentials, stated plainly. Landscape architects on staff, the hardscape systems you're certified to install and how long you've worked these towns.

Where templates fall short for landscape firms

  • Summer all year. A template homepage built around one hero photo shows June in January. A site planned for the seasons shows the work as it looks now.
  • Design-build and maintenance mixed together. One contact form for everything means a full backyard redesign and a mowing request land in the same inbox, with the same questions.
  • Galleries without captions. Photos with no word about the problem, the site or the materials don't show that you solved anything.
  • Stock gardens. Customers can tell a photo of an English estate from a western-suburbs backyard, and they wonder why you didn't show your own.

What the Wexmoor concept shows

The homepage splits visitors three ways right away: design and build, garden care or a paid site visit. The Work page tells five projects as problem, plan and materials. The Approach page explains each step with its fee and its season, then ends with two separate forms, one for design projects and one for care, so each request reaches the right person.

Questions landscape firms ask

How much does a website for a landscape design firm cost?

An Elimenti website for a landscape design firm starts at $4,000, on WordPress or custom-coded. The main variables are how many project pages you need and whether design-build and maintenance need separate sections. Copywriting and a basic SEO setup are included. You get the exact price in writing before work starts.

Can the site separate design-build inquiries from maintenance requests?

Yes. We give each service its own pages and its own short form, so a full redesign inquiry and a seasonal care request don't land in the same pile. Each form asks what that team needs to know: town, scope and budget for design-build; address, start date and who built the garden for care.

Should we list a design fee on our website?

If you charge one, yes. Homeowners who see the fee up front, and whether it's credited toward the install, arrive at the site visit already expecting it. You don't need to publish install pricing. A sentence on how design is billed, plus a typical range for full projects, filters calls without boxing you in.

Can we show projects across seasons?

Yes, and you should if you have the photos. A garden that looks good in February as well as June makes a stronger case than one shot on a single summer day. Each project page can hold photos by season. On WordPress, you add new ones yourself as you shoot them, or Site Care adds them, usually within five business days.

What if we don't have good photos of our work?

Use the best you have and add more over time. Photography is available as an add-on at $85 an hour, and we'll plan shoots around when your projects look their best. If you already work with a landscape photographer, we design around their images instead.

Will the website help us show up in searches for towns like Hinsdale and Burr Ridge?

It helps. Every Elimenti site includes a basic SEO setup: page titles, clean headings, structured data that tells search engines and assistants what you do and where, a sitemap and fast load times. Project pages that name the town add real local relevance. We don't sell monthly SEO retainers or link building.
