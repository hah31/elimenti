Collector car storage website design

A collector car storage website has to answer what an owner asks before handing over the keys: how the building is secured, how the air is kept dry, when they can reach the car and who touches it in between. Elimenti designs websites for storage facilities, car condos, garage condo communities and motor clubs that store members' cars, anywhere in the US, on WordPress or custom-coded. We haven't built one for a storage facility yet. Our closest work is the site we built for 1 of 1 Car Club, a private Porsche club.

What an owner checks before trusting a facility

Two lists written for owners make a good outline for your facility page. Hagerty's guide to choosing storage starts with a brick or concrete building that holds a stable temperature and stays dry, then fire suppression and security. Chubb's storage checklist says a specialized facility typically has limited access, background checks for employees, central fire and burglar detection, a computerized inventory, and humidity and temperature control. Answer each point in plain words:

  • Security. Gates, cameras, alarms, who monitors them and whether staff are on site.
  • Climate and humidity. Heated, cooled or both, and the humidity range you hold. "Climate controlled" alone tells little to someone whose car has a wood frame or original leather.
  • Fire protection. Sprinklers or another suppression system, and who gets the alert.
  • Access. Hours, how much notice you need and whether owners can come and go alone.
  • Who moves the car. Valet or self-park, and whether one car is ever shuffled to free another.
  • Insurance. What the owner's policy must cover and what yours does. They will ask, so say it first.
  • Power. An outlet at each space for a battery tender, and charging for electric cars if you offer it.

Three kinds of business, three kinds of site

"Car storage" covers businesses that sell different things, and each website leads with something different.

  • Storage by the month. Lead with the building, the care and the rate. One Drivers Club's Chicago page puts its address, hours, a starting monthly rate and the full service list on a single page.
  • Units for sale or lease. This is real estate, so the site needs listings. AutoMotorPlex shows each resale garage with its dimensions, electrical service, doors and drains, most with an asking price, and its ownership page lays out five steps from first inquiry to finished build-out.
  • A club with storage. Membership comes first, and storage is one of its benefits. OTTO Car Club in Scottsdale explains its three options (main floor, stacker suites and private garages) and names the membership that includes them.

Plenty of facilities are two of these at once. Then the homepage's first task is sending each visitor down the right path in one click.

Availability, floor plans and unit sizes

Behind every inquiry is one question: do you have room for my car? The site should answer it without a phone call.

  • One entry per unit or space type. Width, depth, ceiling height, door size, power and a status: available, reserved or sold. Changing a status is one edit.
  • A floor plan that reads on a phone. A site plan showing where each unit sits, with every unit linked to its entry.
  • A date on it. "Updated November 12" tells a buyer how far to trust the plan.
  • A waitlist when you're full. A short form asking what they'd store, and when, keeps that demand on file.

Care services, listed like a menu

Battery tending, regular start-ups, tire pressure checks, detailing, transport and seasonal prep separate a collector facility from a storage unit. List each service and say three things about it: whether the monthly rate includes it, what it costs if not and who does the work. If mechanical or paint work goes to outside specialists, say so.

Clients who already store with you need a different page: a service request form for a wash, a pickup or a car ready by Friday. It goes to your staff inbox or links to the scheduling tool you already use.

An inquiry form that asks about the car

A form with a name, an email and a message box leaves you writing back for the basics. A better one collects what you need to quote:

  • What they want. Monthly storage, a unit to buy or lease, membership or a tour.
  • The cars. Year, make and model, how many, and anything unusually long, low or tall.
  • Timing. The move-in date and the length of stay: one winter, a year or open-ended.
  • How it's used. Driven most weekends or put away for the season.
  • Transport. Driving it in or needing it collected.

The page then says what happens next: who replies, how soon and that a tour comes before any contract.

What stays off the site

A storage website advertises a building full of valuable cars, so privacy is part of the design.

  • Clients' cars, plates and names. Nothing appears without the owner's written yes. Photos of the empty space, or of your own cars, do the job.
  • Security specifics. Say you have cameras and monitored alarms. Leave out camera positions, the monitoring company and the hours nobody is there.
  • An inventory. No list of what's inside, and no photo wide enough to serve as one.
  • Location data in photos. A phone can save where a picture was taken inside the file. We strip that from every image before it's published.

Local search basics

Owners search by city. A Google Business Profile, with the same name, address and phone number the website shows, puts you on the map. Google's guidelines ask for your actual street address and don't accept a P.O. box. Each building also gets its own page with hours, trailer parking and directions from the nearest highway. A facility that opens by appointment only says so there.

The clubhouse and events

A lounge, a calendar or a monthly cars-and-coffee often sells a facility better than any feature list. Each event gets a date, a time, a line on who's invited and photos afterward. Car show websites covers event pages, and car club websites covers applications and members-only areas.

Questions storage owners ask

Should a storage facility publish its monthly rate?

A starting rate saves calls from people it was never going to suit. One Drivers Club's Chicago page lists monthly storage as beginning at $685, including taxes and fees (checked November 18, 2026). If your rate depends on the car and the services, publish a starting figure and say what moves it.

Can we update availability ourselves?

Yes. On a WordPress site, staff log in and switch a unit from available to reserved themselves. On a custom-coded site, you email the change and Site Care publishes it within five business days, so WordPress suits a facility whose availability changes often.

Can people reserve or pay for storage through the website?

They can request a space through a form. We don't build payment systems. If you already bill through a booking or invoicing tool, the site links to it, and contracts are still signed with you directly.

We sell garage condos and rent storage. Is that one website or two?

Usually one. Buyers and renters want the same proof about the building, and one domain is easier to keep current. The homepage splits the two paths at the top, and each has its own pages and its own form.

Do you work with facilities outside Illinois?

Yes. We're based in Hinsdale, Illinois, and every project runs over video calls, so we take facilities anywhere in the US. Outside the Chicago area, we design around photos from your own photographer.
