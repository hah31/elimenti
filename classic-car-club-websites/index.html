Websites for classic and marque car clubs

A club with a long history carries decades on its website: chapters, events, a magazine archive, a technical library and a member directory, all kept up by volunteers who change every few years. Elimenti designs and rebuilds websites for national and regional clubs devoted to one make or a family of makes, anywhere in the US, usually on WordPress, including clubs moving off Joomla, Drupal or a custom-built site.

What an established club's site has to handle

  • Public pages and members' pages. Prospective members, the press and sponsors see the club at its best. Dues-paying members log in for the rest.
  • Joining and renewing. Join and renewal have to work with the membership system the club already uses, so the treasurer isn't keying in records twice. How the two connect is scoped in the proposal.
  • Chapters. Each with its own officers, contacts and events, inside one site that still looks like one club.
  • A calendar for the whole club. National meets, tours and chapter events in one place, with registration and the details people check before they drive three hours.
  • A member directory. For members only, so owners of the same model can find each other.
  • The magazine and newsletter archive. Every back issue the club has, findable by year and issue.
  • A technical library. Manuals and tech articles, organized by model and year.
  • The club's own history. When and how it started, past presidents, award winners and the cars that defined it.

Chapters run by their own volunteers

A regional chapter shouldn't have to email the national webmaster to post a tech session. Each chapter gets its own pages for officers, news and events, and its volunteers get their own logins to keep them current. Chapter events also show on the club-wide calendar, so a member traveling for a national meet can see what the local chapter has on that weekend. The design stays the same across every chapter, so the club looks like one organization, not a collection of separate sites.

Archives members actually use

Decades of back issues and tech articles are often the most valuable thing a club owns, and the hardest thing to find on its website. They work best organized the way members search: magazines by year and issue, and the library by model, year and system, with a search box over all of it. The club decides which parts are public, to show what membership gets you, and which stay members-only.

A history page belongs here too. A club that has run for decades has a founding story, past presidents and award winners worth keeping in one place, and new members want to know them.

A member directory that stays inside the club

The directory sits behind each member's own login and never appears on a public page or in search results. Members choose whether to be listed, update their own entry, including their chapter and the cars they own, and search by chapter or model to find someone with the same car. It stays current in a way a printed roster can't.

Moving off Joomla, Drupal or a custom-built site

Plenty of club sites run on Joomla, Drupal or code a member wrote years ago, and those systems still work. The usual problem is that only one or two volunteers know how the club's site is put together, and when they step back, updates stop. Moving to WordPress puts editing within reach of every volunteer who needs it. The move runs in steps:

  • Take inventory. Pages, articles, magazine files, past events, forum posts, member accounts and every address the old site uses.
  • Decide what comes across. Some content moves as it is, some is archived and some is retired. The club makes those calls with us.
  • Move the content. Articles and pages into WordPress, and back issues and library files organized by year and model.
  • Bring members over. Member records come across from the membership system or the old site. Passwords can't always move between systems, so members may be asked to set a new one the first time they log in.
  • Redirect every old address. Each old page points permanently to its new one, so links in past newsletters, forum threads and search results still land somewhere useful.
  • Hand it to the volunteers. A walkthrough for each person who'll edit, written documentation of how the site is put together and admin access for the club, with every account in the club's name.

Moving old content is scoped and priced separately in the proposal, because one club's archive is fifty pages and another's is twenty years of back issues.

Why WordPress usually fits a club

  • The next webmaster can pick it up. WordPress is common enough that whoever volunteers next has likely used it, and any WordPress developer can step in.
  • Standard plugins, no lock-in. Memberships, events and forums run on widely used plugins rather than code only one person understands. Any premium licenses are in the club's name.
  • Updates handled. WordPress needs regular updates to stay current. Site Care covers them, plus backups, on the monthly plan, so that job doesn't land on a volunteer.

Questions clubs ask

Can you move our Joomla or Drupal site to WordPress?

Yes. We take stock of what the current site holds, agree with the club on what moves, carry it across and redirect every old address to its new page. Member records come over too, though members may need to set a new password the first time they log in.

Will links in old newsletters and forum posts still work?

Yes. Every old address is redirected permanently to the page that replaces it, so links in past issues, forum threads and search results still land somewhere useful.

Can the member directory stay members-only?

Yes. It sits behind each member's own login and never shows on public pages. Members choose whether they're listed and keep their own entry up to date.

Can our volunteers update the site?

Yes. On WordPress, each volunteer who needs it gets their own login, and at launch we walk them through how to edit their part of the site: events, chapter news, posts and photos. The club also gets written documentation, so the next volunteer isn't starting from scratch.

Can you work with our existing membership system or CRM?

That's where we start. Joining and renewing should run through the system the club already keeps its records in. How the website connects to it depends on the system, so we look at it before the proposal and scope the connection there, with the price in writing. If a system can't connect the way the club needs, we'll say so before anything is signed.

How is moving our old content priced?

Separately from the new site, in the same written proposal. We look at what the current site holds, such as library files, past events and forum posts, agree with the club on what moves, and price that work on its own line before anything starts.

How pricing works

Every project is scoped individually, and the exact price and timeline come in a written proposal before any work starts. Copywriting, a basic SEO setup and up to three rounds of revisions are included. Membership connections and moving old content each get their own line in that proposal. We work with clubs anywhere in the US, over video calls.
